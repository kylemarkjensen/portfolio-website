NeuroMark

A major theme of much of my research has been centered around the NeuroMark initiative, aimed towards establishing a common reference space across neuroimaging analyses. I've contributed to the development of several NeuroMark templates (see NeuroMark Templates), but my most notable contribution has been towards the development of the NeuroMark Multi-Scale Functional Brain Atlas. This work builds upon the brilliant work of Dr. Iraji and colleagues, expanding their valuable neuroimaging resource by manually and meticulously organizing and describing data-derived brain networks from more than 100,000 resting-state fMRI scans in a way that is hopefully more familiar and accessible to a broader neuroimaging community. To date, this resource has been used in at least 40 studies and I hope that it will continue to be useful for helping to standardize results and expand our understanding of the functional brain (read more about it here).

Schizophrenia and Psychosis

Another common theme of my research can be described as efforts towards better identifying and understanding psychiatric and neurological disorders. Many of my publications have focused specifically on schizophrenia and psychosis, employing the data-driven NeuroMark approach to identify consistent patterns of disrupted network activity in resting-state fMRI.
